scoreThe long-range score

\(\mathrm{LRS} = 100\left(\prod_j s_j\right)^{1/n}\)
how the score is defined, and what it deliberately refuses to do
$$\mathrm{LRS} = 100\left(\prod_{j} s_j^{\,w_j}\right)^{1/\sum_j w_j},\qquad s_j \in [0,1],\; 1 = \text{correct}$$

Four quantities, each with a value that is right by construction, each mapped so that 1 is correct and 0 is the no-long-range floor: the fraction of the \(r\)-dependence reproduced, how much of the physical magnitude is captured, the reply to a distant charge, and the offset-free error measured against what a flat model would score. The geometric mean is the point — a model that scores zero on any one of them has no long-range physics, and credit elsewhere should not disguise that.

The bars beside each score are the four components in that order, so the number is never shown without the thing it was built from. A score marked * was built from fewer than four — a task that did not run cannot be told from one that was passed, so those are not comparable with the rest. This summarises these tests on these sets; it is not a ranking of models in general. The ceiling row is the dataset's own multipole + induction prior, not a model. Click a column to re-sort, and the dot beside a model to take it out of every widget below — the table and the figures always show the same set.

Weights are a question, not a setting. Changing them changes what is being asked, so the formula above restates itself and every score and rank follows. Equal weights are the default because no component has a principled claim to more; a weight of zero removes the term rather than shrinking it, which is why taking a task out and setting its weight to zero are the same operation.

group A — the far field

AAsymptotics

What a charged pair does once the fragments are far enough apart that nothing local can reach between them. The reference is not a fitted curve but an asymptotic law, so a model either reproduces it or it does not. The protocol below is the scan every task in this group is measured on.

protocol A · the dimer scan — drag the separation, the cutoff and the gate
A · the dimer scan — two fragments pulled apart along one axis. Everything the tail diagnostics measure happens in this picture.

A · Asymptotics — the far field

A1Asymptotic decay of the interaction energy

the quantity, and why this is the right one
$$E_{\mathrm{int}}(r)\;\xrightarrow{\;r\to\infty\;}\;\textcolor{#0072B2}{\frac{q_A q_B}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}}\;+\;\textcolor{#D55E00}{O(r^{-2})}$$
the monopole term — all that survives for a charged pairdipole and higher, which die away faster

Beyond the cutoff a charged pair is governed by one term, and it does not decay quickly. Separation runs along x and the signed error up y, on a diverging scale centred on zero, so white is exact and the two hues are the two directions of being wrong. An error that grows with separation is the long-range failure; one that shrinks is ordinary short-range inaccuracy.

A · Asymptotics — the far field

A2Residuals against the reference

the quantity, and why this is the right one
$$\textcolor{#0072B2}{E^{\mathrm{model}}_{\mathrm{int}}}\;\;\text{vs}\;\;\textcolor{#D55E00}{E^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{\mathrm{int}}},\qquad\textcolor{#009E73}{y = x}$$
the model, on the vertical axisthe reference, on the horizontalthe identity line: agreement

A model can sit on the identity line at contact and leave it in the tail, which is exactly what the summary statistics are built to catch. Colour is separation, so the departure orders itself by distance; shape and colour both carry the class on the next view.

A · Asymptotics — the far field

A3Parity between two models

the quantity, and why this is the right one
$$\textcolor{#0072B2}{E^{A}_{\mathrm{int}}}\;\;\text{vs}\;\;\textcolor{#D55E00}{E^{B}_{\mathrm{int}}}$$
model on the x axis — choose it belowmodel on the y axis — choose it below

Pick the model on each axis. Points on the identity line are structures the two agree on; the spread away from it is where they differ. Two models that fail on the same structures fail for a reason in the data; two that fail on different ones fail for reasons of their own. Class is carried by both shape and colour here, so the legend is readable either way.

group B — the reply to a distant charge

BResponse

A charge is placed far from a pair of local arrangements, and the energies are differenced twice. Composition and total charge are identical in all four terms, so every offset cancels and only the long-range reply survives — a quantity a Coulomb tail alone cannot produce.

protocol B · the fixed-charge quartet — drag the charge distance and the cutoff
B · the fixed-charge quartet — a local pair of sites, and one charge far away. Four energies, differenced twice, so that everything except the long-range reply cancels.

B · Response — the reply to a distant charge

B1Fixed-charge response

the quantity, and why this is the right one
$$\Delta\Delta E=\textcolor{#0072B2}{\big[E(s_1,\text{near})-E(s_0,\text{near})\big]}-\textcolor{#D55E00}{\big[E(s_1,\text{far})-E(s_0,\text{far})\big]}$$
the local energy difference with the charge nearbythe same difference with it far away — subtracting this removes everything local

Four structures of identical composition and identical total charge, so every per-element offset, every charge-state offset and any constant cancels exactly. What survives is the reply of a local energy difference to a charge placed far away, and it decays as \(R^{-2}\) — a question a Coulomb tail alone cannot answer.

group C — charges and moments

CElectrostatic variables

The electrostatic variables themselves: whether a model's atomic charges sum to the charge it was given, and how they compare with the reference partitioning. A model can have a plausible energy and implausible charges.

C · Electrostatic variables — charges and moments

C1Charge conservation and the Löwdin reference

the quantity, and why this is the right one
$$\textcolor{#0072B2}{\sum_{i\in f} q_i} \;=\; \textcolor{#D55E00}{Q_f}\qquad\text{and}\qquad \mu \;=\; \sum_i q_i \mathbf{r}_i$$
the model's atomic charges, summed over a fragmentthe fragment's formal charge — an integer, by construction

Every atom of every scored dimer: the DFT Löwdin charge against the formal charge it was assigned. The formal charges are integers, so they lie on a few horizontal lines; the real charges spread along the identity line, and that spread is the polarisation and charge transfer a model has to reproduce. Each point is one atomic environment, so selecting one highlights that atom.

DMany-body

What is left after every pair contribution is removed. A potential assembled from pairs has no access to this term however well it does on dimers.

method

Protocol and conventions

how every figure on this page was produced, and the encoding rules

No model was re-evaluated for these figures. Every point comes from the canonical records and the per-term energies saved by lrbench eval --save-predictions, recombined through each group's own definition — so a model's value and its reference are the same linear combination and a point compares like with like. Records flagged as DFT pathologies are excluded. Energies in meV.

Encoding. A class is an identity, so it is carried by marker shape, never by a colour ramp: chemiscope's palettes are continuous, and reading categories off a continuous bar is exactly what good practice forbids. A signed error is carried by a diverging palette centred on zero, so white means no error and the two hues are the two directions of being wrong. A magnitude — a separation, an absolute error — is carried by a perceptually uniform sequential ramp.

Levels of theory are never pooled. A model trained at a different level from the reference data is marked ⚠ in the table: its energies are not comparable with these references, its dimensionless diagnostics are.
